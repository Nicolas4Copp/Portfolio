SAIBER

Sistema Automático de Irrigação à Base de Energia Renovável
Renewable Energy-Powered Automated Irrigation System

Back

Developed in 2024 as a collaborative project with my classmates at InediTec School, this project was designed to address the needs of people who spend extended periods away from home and require a reliable solution to keep their plants properly watered and cared for.
The project consists of a low-cost, sustainable automated irrigation system that also monitors soil pH levels, helping users maintain suitable conditions for plant growth while reducing the need for constant manual maintenance.

Material and components

  • 2 Solar Panels;
  • 1 Arduino Micro Board;
  • 1 Traffic Light Module;
  • 1 Soil Moisture Sensor;
  • 1 12V Water Pump;
  • 1 pH Sensor (Prototype);
  • 1 Battery Charging Module;

Development Process

We connected two solar panels to a backup battery system, integrating them with the irrigation motor to provide the power needed to operate the pump. Additionally, we assembled an Arduino-based system with a traffic light indicator to monitor and display soil moisture levels.

We also developed the concept and initial programming for a pH sensor designed to collect soil pH measurements and send the data to a custom-built mobile application.

Note:

The pH monitoring and mobile application integration remained at the theoretical and prototype stages, as the project was not continued to full implementation.

Scientific Exhibition

As a result of our work on the SAIBER project, our supervising teachers selected our team to present it at MOSCLING (Scientific Exhibition of the Northern Coast of Rio Grande do Sul).

During the event, we had the opportunity to showcase the system's functionalities and present our work to representatives of the Federal University of Rio Grande do Sul (UFRGS).

Mobile App Prototype

This is the initial prototype of the mobile application designed to complement the SAIBER project.

Both the visual design and programming are intentionally simple, as this is a low-fidelity prototype. Its main purpose was to demonstrate the application's potential features during the science fair and illustrate how it would integrate with the fully developed irrigation system.

The prototype served as a visual and functional concept, helping communicate how users could interact with the system and access information collected by its sensors.

The photograph showcases our SAIBER project, featuring the Arduino enclosure, solar panels, and pepper plant. A smartphone displaying the app prototype completes the setup, demonstrating how the hardware and software were designed to work together.

Personal Growth and Development

This application is a great example of how much I have grown as a designer and developer when compared to my more recent projects.

At the time, I was just beginning my journey in design and programming. Although I am still developing my skills and have not yet established myself as a professional, I have gained valuable experience and significantly improved my technical knowledge since then.

Looking back at this project allows me to recognize my progress, understand how far I have come, and appreciate the continuous learning process behind my development.
